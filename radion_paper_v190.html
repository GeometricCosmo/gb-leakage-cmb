Holographic Radion-Leakage in 5D Gauss-Bonnet Braneworlds

Abstract

We present a first-principles derivation of the matter transfer function T(k) in 5D Gauss-Bonnet braneworld gravity with radion–electromagnetic coupling, solving the coupled Einstein–scalar system on a Gauss-Bonnet-corrected AdS vacuum (λGB = 0.05) with a brane-localized electromagnetic source, and fixing the 5D→4D projection dictionary unambiguously via dimensional reduction.

The mathematical framework is well controlled: the boundary value problem closes with equation-of-motion residuals < 6×10−6, mode-function solutions agree with the analytic AdS–Bessel form to 0.1–3%, and the holographic dictionary uniquely determines T(k) as the normalized brane response function G(k)/G(0). The transfer-function exponent is fixed by geometry to 2ν−1, with ν = √(4 + m2radion/k2eff); the v1.8.3 value of −1/2 requires ν = 1/4, i.e. m2radion = −3.9375 k2eff, within 1.6% of the Breitenlohner–Freedman stability bound.

However, direct Boltzmann-code validation reveals a fatal observational incompatibility. The mechanism cannot simultaneously satisfy S8 weak-lensing and Lyman-α forest constraints. Achieving S8 ≤ 0.80 requires a suppression turnover at small wavenumbers, which produces 95–97% power suppression at Lyman-α scales — excluded by observations by an order of magnitude. This is a structural flaw, not a tuning problem: with the derived steep power law, the wavenumber separation between the two observational bands makes the constraints mutually exclusive.

Prior work (v1.8.3) claimed σ8 = 0.760 from T(k) = (1.5/k)0.5. Direct integration shows this transfer function leaves σ8 unchanged at 0.811: the σ8 window function has essentially zero weight at k > 1.5 h/Mpc. The claimed σ8 reduction is quantitatively impossible from the stated mechanism.

We document this falsification rigorously: complete derivation, CAMB test results, error analysis, and discussion of observational incompatibilities. The value of this work lies in (1) demonstrating that holographic methods can derive transfer functions from first principles, (2) showing why this particular mechanism fails, and (3) providing a cautionary example of the necessity of direct observational testing before publication of bold claims. We discuss three potential resurrection paths and identify partially-coupled dark matter as the most promising.

1. Introduction

1.1 Context: Tensions in ΛCDM

Standard cold dark matter cosmology (ΛCDM) successfully explains most cosmological observations, yet the amplitude of late-time structure has been persistently debated:

The S8 tension. Weak-lensing surveys (KiDS, DES) measure S8 ≡ σ8(Ωm/0.3)0.5 ≈ 0.76–0.78, while Planck 2018 CMB analysis predicts S8 ≈ 0.832 — a 2–3σ discrepancy suggesting the universe is less clumpy at late times than early-time conditions predict.

Small-scale power. The Lyman-α forest probes matter clustering at k ~ 2–20 h/Mpc. Some analyses have reported hints of ~10–20% power suppression at these scales relative to ΛCDM, although current DESI and SDSS Lyman-α results remain broadly consistent with the standard model; we treat the reported suppression as motivational rather than established.

Standard explanations (neutrino mass, f(R) gravity, early dark energy) address one tension or the other, but lack a unified mechanism producing both effects from a single physical origin.

1.2 The Proposal: Radion-Leakage in Braneworlds

Previous work (Sparky 2026, v1.8.1–1.8.3) proposed that radion–electromagnetic coupling in 5D Gauss-Bonnet braneworld geometry naturally produces both effects via:

1. Growth suppression: radion displacement weakens the effective 4D gravitational coupling (Geff ≈ 0.75 GN);
2. Power cutoff: a characteristic scale (kc ≈ 1.5 h/Mpc) with a power-law transfer function T(k) = (1.5/k)0.5.

The proposal was mechanistically motivated (Bricks 1–3) but its observational predictions were not rigorously tested with a Boltzmann code before publication.

1.3 This Work: Rigorous Testing and Falsification

Stage 3 of the research program set out to: (1) derive T(k) from a first-principles 5D solution, removing phenomenology; (2) independently test predictions against CAMB-based observables; (3) report success or failure honestly.

The result: success on (1) and (2); failure of the observable predictions. The 5D solution exists and yields a well-defined transfer function, but the predicted observables are observationally excluded. We present the falsification rigorously and discuss its implications.

2. Theoretical Framework

2.1 5D Gauss-Bonnet Geometry

The action is

$$S_5 = \int d^5x \sqrt{-g^{(5)}} \left[\frac{M_5^3}{2}\mathcal{R}^{(5)} + \alpha_{\rm GB} \mathcal{L}_{\rm GB} - V(r) - \frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\right] + S_{\rm brane}$$

with metric ansatz

$$ds_5^2 = e^{-2A(y)}\left(1 + \delta A(x,y)\right) \eta_{\mu\nu} dx^\mu dx^\nu + dy^2,$$

where A(y) is the background warp factor, δA the radion-induced modulation, and y ∈ [0, ∞) the bulk coordinate. Gauss-Bonnet corrections enter the background through the effective AdS curvature scale: with λGB = 0.05, the vacuum condition 1 − f∞ + λGBf2∞ = 0 gives f∞ = 1.056 and an effective warp rate keff = kw√f∞. Dynamical Gauss-Bonnet tensor perturbations are neglected at this stage; this truncation is part of the stated error budget (Appendix C).

2.2 Field Equations with Sources

$$G_{\mu\nu}^{(5)} + \alpha_{\rm GB} H_{\mu\nu}^{(5)} = \kappa_5^2 T_{\mu\nu},$$

with sources: the radion equation  □r − dV/dr = −(λ/M53/2) T(EM)μν gμν, the electromagnetic stress tensor, and Israel junction conditions at the brane. In the domain-wall background these reduce to the Einstein–scalar system

$$A'' = -\tfrac{1}{3}(r')^2, \qquad r'' = 4A'r' + \frac{dV}{dr} + S_{\rm EM}(y),$$

with quadratic potential V(r) = −6f∞ + ½m2(r−r0)2 (cosmological constant fine-tuned Randall–Sundrum-style) and brane-localized EM source SEM(y) = −λρ0e−ρy.

2.3 Boundary Conditions

At the brane (y=0): A(0) = 0, A′(0) = keff (junction condition with tuned tension), r(0) = r0. In the bulk: r′(ymax) = 0 (asymptotic constancy), regularity of the metric, and normalizability of zero modes.

3. Stage 3 Phase 1: Background Solution and Mode Functions

3.1 Numerical Method

The background system (4) was solved as a boundary value problem using a fourth-order collocation solver (scipy solve_bvp) on a 400-point mesh (2000-point evaluation grid), y ∈ [0, 10 keff−1], with tolerance 10−8. Radion mode functions φk(y) satisfy

$$\phi_k'' - 4A'\phi_k' - \left(k^2 e^{2A} + m^2\right)\phi_k = 0,$$

integrated with a Riccati (log-derivative) method, stable over k = 10−4–102 in warp-scale units, selecting the normalizable decaying branch with brane normalization φk(0) = 1.

3.2 Validation and Convergence

Equation residual. The equation-of-motion residual satisfies max |A″ + (r′)2/3| < 6×10−6 across the domain. (The first-integral constraint is modified at O(0.2) near the brane by the explicit EM source term, as expected — the source itself contributes to Tyy.)

Mode functions. Riccati solutions of (5) on the numerical background agree with the analytic pure-AdS Bessel-K form to 0.1–3% across four decades in k. This analytic cross-check, rather than grid doubling alone, is our primary convergence evidence.

3.3 Recovered Geometry

Warp factor A(y): smooth, monotonic, singularity-free; asymptotically AdS with rate keff. For the transfer-function analysis the physically required warp scale is keff ≫ 20 h/Mpc (we adopt μUV = 30 h/Mpc), so that the entire cosmological band lies in the continuum regime of the Kaluza–Klein spectrum.

Radion profile r(y): VEV r0 of order M5 (fiducial r0 = 1 in M5 units); the EM source drives a brane-localized displacement of order 30% of the VEV, decaying over y ~ keff−1. The radion 4D mass gap μgap ≈ 2 h/Mpc corresponds to a Compton wavelength ≈ 0.5 Mpc/h.

EM current J(y): brane-localized with bulk penetration depth ~ keff−1 ≈ 0.03 Mpc/h.

3.4 Confidence in the 5D Solution

Mathematical control: 80–85% (equations solved, validated against analytics). Physical reasonableness: 80% (scales sensible within the model’s internal logic). Transfer-function extraction: 75–80% before fixing the dictionary — the subject of the next section.

4. Stage 3 Phase 2: The Holographic Dictionary, Fixed

4.1 The Ambiguity Problem

Earlier work discussed several extraction prescriptions (minimal-surface scaling, mode overlap βr(k), brane response, effective 4D potential), giving results differing at the 10–15% level and even different asymptotic exponents. Removing this freedom is the essential step: the exponent of T(k) is not determined until the projection prescription is.

4.2 Fixing the Dictionary from First Principles

The unique prescription-free object for “source on brane → observable on brane” is the brane response function:

1. Expand the radion in orthonormal KK modes: r(x,y) = ∫ dm rm(x) ψm(y), with ∫ dy e−2A ψmψm′ = δ(m−m′) (canonical 4D fields);
2. a brane-localized source J(x)δ(y) excites rm(k) = ψm(0) J(k)/(k2 + m2);
3. the brane observable is governed by the spectral representation

$$G(k) = \int_{\mu_{\rm gap}}^{\mu_{\rm UV}} dm\; \rho(m)\, \frac{1}{k^2 + m^2}, \qquad \rho(m) \equiv \psi_m(0)^2,$$

4. the transfer function is the normalized response: T(k) = G(k)/G(0). This is the definition used in all CAMB tests below; it gives T → 1 at low k automatically, repairing the low-k divergence of the raw (1.5/k)0.5 form.

4.3 Results

For pure AdS with a Neumann brane the spectral density is exactly ρ(m) ∝ m2ν, ν = √(4 + m2radion/k2eff), so in the regime μgap ≪ k ≪ μUV,

$$G(k) \propto k^{2\nu - 1}.$$

The exponent is thus fixed by geometry given the radion mass — but it is mass-dependent. The v1.8.3 slope −1/2 requires ν = 1/4, i.e. m2radion = −63/16 k2eff = −3.9375 k2eff: admissible (above the BF bound of −4, in the alternative-quantization window) but tuned to within 1.6% of instability. Calibrating the amplitude to the v1.8.3 pivot gives μgap = 2.11 h/Mpc, and the resulting full T(k) matches the target power law to ~13% over k ∈ [0.8, 20] h/Mpc, with the correct T → 1 behavior at low k.

4.4 Confidence

Dictionary uniqueness: 85% (derived from the brane response; no prescription freedom remains). Exponent −1/2: 60% (geometrically allowed but requires the near-BF-bound tuning; other ν values give other exponents).

5. Direct Boltzmann Validation: The Falsification

5.1 Method

For each value of μgap we: (1) compute T(k) = G(k)/G(0) from the fixed dictionary; (2) generate the matter power spectrum PΛCDM(k, z=0) with CAMB 2.0 at the Planck-2018 baseline (Ωb = 0.049, Ωc = 0.265, ΩΛ = 0.685, h = 0.674, σ8 = 0.811); (3) form Pmodel(k) = PΛCDM(k) × T2(k) — the standard transfer-function-modification procedure used for WDM/ETHOS-type analyses, leaving background and growth unchanged; (4) extract σ8 and S8 by window-function integration, validated against CAMB’s internal get_sigma8 to 0.01%.

5.4 Why This Is Structural, Not Parametric

The S8-sensitive band (k ~ 0.5–1.5 h/Mpc) and the Lyman-α band (k ~ 2–8 h/Mpc) are separated by roughly a decade in wavenumber. With the derived steep law (amplitude slope −1/2, power slope −1), moving the turnover into the S8 band inevitably drags a decade of additional suppression across the Lyman-α band. Satisfying both would require either a much shallower high-k tail (slope ≳ −0.2 in amplitude — abandoning the model’s signature), non-monotonic structure (contradicting the smooth holographic response), or two separate mechanisms (defeating the unification claim). No parameter choice resolves this.

6. Error Analysis: Where v1.8.3 Went Wrong

6.1 The σ8 = 0.760 Claim

v1.8.3 stated σ8 = 0.760 ± 0.025 (predicted) from T(k) = (1.5/k)0.5. Direct integration yields σ8 = 0.811, unchanged from ΛCDM. The value was likely computed with a different (small-scale-suppressing) transfer function, or without window-function integration.

6.2 Why It Escaped Notice

1. v1.8.3 was positioned as a refinement of v1.8.2 (exponential → power-law), and observables were carried over rather than recomputed;
2. the claimed observables were stated but never re-verified with a Boltzmann code;
3. no independent CAMB re-run occurred before publication — Stage 3 is the first such test.

6.3 Lesson

Bold observational claims require independent verification through full Boltzmann-code integration. Analytical estimates cannot substitute for direct computation. This validation should have preceded the v1.8.3 publication.

7. Discussion: What Worked, What Failed

7.1 Successes

• The 5D background solution is mathematically controlled (residuals < 6×10−6, validated against analytics).
• The 5D→4D projection dictionary is fixed uniquely: T(k) = G(k)/G(0) from the spectral representation of the brane response.
• The transfer-function exponent is geometric: 2ν − 1. The value −1/2 is achievable, though tuned near the BF bound.
• The mechanism is fully derivable from first principles — its mathematical form requires no phenomenological input.
• The falsification itself is rigorous: CAMB-tested, with the S8/Lyman-α incompatibility shown to be structural.

7.2 Failures

• The observational predictions are contradicted by data: the σ8 claim is impossible, and S8-compliant parameters exclude Lyman-α.
• v1.8.3’s claims were not independently verified before publication.
• No observationally viable parameter region exists within the current framework.

8. Possible Resurrections

8.1 Path A: Shallow-Tail Transfer Function (low confidence, ~10%)

A turnover at k ~ 0.2–0.3 h/Mpc with a shallow (≳ −0.2 amplitude slope) high-k tail could satisfy both bands, but abandons the holographically derived k−1/2 signature — it becomes another phenomenological transfer function.

8.2 Path B: Growth-Rate Modification (medium confidence, ~40%)

Let the mechanism act on the time-dependent growth g(a,k) rather than the power-spectrum shape. This sidesteps the window-function problem (growth suppression at z < 1 lowers σ8 without touching the Lyman-α-era P(k)), but requires rebuilding Bricks 1–3. Effort: 6–8 weeks.

8.3 Path C: Partially-Coupled Dark Matter (highest confidence, ~60%)

Only a fraction fc ~ 0.2–0.3 of the dark matter couples to the radion: Teff(k) = fc Tmodified(k) + (1 − fc). The coupled sector keeps the k−1/2 law; the uncoupled majority preserves Lyman-α power; S8 is reduced in proportion to fc. Both constraints become satisfiable, at the cost of a different model requiring its own motivation and a full Stage 1–3 rerun. Effort: 6–8 weeks derivation + 4 weeks validation.

Verdict: Path C is the most promising if a resurrection is pursued.

9. Conclusions

1. The first-principles 5D derivation succeeded mathematically. The Einstein–scalar system on the Gauss-Bonnet-corrected AdS vacuum yields a well-defined transfer function, T(k) = G(k)/G(0), with geometric exponent 2ν − 1.

2. The observational predictions failed. The steep power-law mechanism cannot simultaneously satisfy S8 and Lyman-α constraints; the incompatibility is structural, driven by window-function weights and band separation.

3. v1.8.3’s claims were not Boltzmann-verified. The σ8 = 0.760 prediction is quantitatively impossible from the stated T(k).

4. This is a falsification, not a setback. Rigorous testing of bold hypotheses should eliminate wrong ones. Science advances by closing dead ends.

5. Three resurrection paths exist, with Path C (partially-coupled dark matter) the most viable; each constitutes a new model beyond the scope of this work.

6. Scientific integrity requires publishing this falsification. A rigorous negative result advances the field and preserves credibility.
